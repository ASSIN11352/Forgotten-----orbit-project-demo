Who left these on the Moon and Mars?

Real objects left on the Moon and Mars, and why they were sent. Each one was built to answer a question, and some of them still help scientists today.

Let’s look at three of them: a robot lander, a mirror, and a seismometer. Each one tells a different part of the story.

Surveyor 1

A robot landed on the Moon before Apollo

Surveyor 1 was sent ahead of Apollo to check the ground. It was the first spacecraft to make a true soft landing on the Moon, in 1966.

It sent back more than 11,000 photos and data on the surface and temperature. Engineers used what it learned to plan safer landings.

Ask the object

Why leave a mirror on the Moon?

What is it? A square panel of small glass corner mirrors left by Apollo 11 in 1969.

What problem? Measuring the Earth-Moon distance with great precision.

What now? Scientists keep firing lasers at the mirrors and timing the light that bounces back, so research with them continues.

The human part

InSight listened to Mars

Its seismometer, SEIS, measured marsquakes and other ground movement on Mars. It was the first seismometer placed directly on another planet.

By studying how waves travel through the planet, scientists learned more about Mars’ deep interior, from its crust down toward its core.
